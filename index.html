Write‑up

Rationale (Design Decisions)

I focused on one region at a time so patterns wouldn’t get buried by differences in scale between continents. A stacked area chart highlights both overall totals and how each category contributes to them, while the Top-N cutoff keeps the view clear and readable. The brush and zoom make it easy to explore specific stretches of time, and the legend lets you toggle individual components to see their impact. The percent-change option normalizes the data to show relative growth or decline, while the absolute view keeps the raw magnitudes that matter for policy. Tooltips provide exact values and percent changes when needed. I considered alternatives like small multiples and treemaps, but they either made trends harder to follow over time (treemaps) or took too much space for a single-page format (small multiples). Finding a distinct, balanced color scheme for all categories was also a real challenge — it took several iterations to land on one that was both readable and consistent across views.

Development Process

This was a solo project, completed in about 12 hours: around 1 for planning, 1 for data prep, and 10 for coding. Most of the time went into reshaping the quarterly data and getting the brush-zoom to work smoothly with stacked layouts. Once that foundation was stable, I added the percent-change metric and Top-N ranking to make comparisons easier. The color tuning was another tricky step and making sure colors stayed distinct while still fitting the dark background was more difficult than expected. Future improvements could include pinned annotations for notable events (like the 2020 trough), export options for screenshots or CSVs, and remembering the user’s selections through the URL. Also, really fine tuning that color scheme.
